A personal reflection on faith, freedom of conscience, and responsibility

By Tyler Tinney

Personal connection essay

Morality, Promised Lands and Freedom

In his Farewell Address, George Washington emphasized the importance of religion and morality in preserving a free and prosperous nation, stating, "Of all the dispositions and habits which lead to political prosperity, religion and morality are indispensable supports" Morality can be defined as the principles and values that guide individuals in distinguishing right from wrong and choosing to act with honesty, integrity, compassion, and responsibility. Religion helps establish and strengthen these principles by teaching people that they are accountable to God as well as to their fellow citizens. John Adams expressed a similar concern in 1798 when he warned that the Constitution was made for "a moral and religious people" and would be inadequate to govern those whose passions were not restrained by those principles. I believe these warnings closely resemble the teachings of the Book of Mormon, particularly Ether 2:10, which warns that those who possess the promised land must serve God or eventually be "swept off" when they reach the fulness of iniquity. While Washington and Adams warned that abandoning religious morality could lead to the collapse of constitutional government, Ether offers the additional spiritual warning that a sufficiently wicked people could lose the blessings of the land itself. However, the scripture also emphasizes that this judgment does not come until the people have reached the fulness of iniquity, demonstrating God's patience with imperfect people and the opportunity they have to repent. To me, these teachings reinforce the importance of preserving religious freedom and encouraging moral conduct, not only for the benefit of individuals but also for the continued prosperity and liberty of the nation.

Growing up in an environment where my religious beliefs as a member of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints were not widely shared by my peers presented several moral challenges. One of the most difficult was balancing my desire for friendship and acceptance with my desire to remain faithful to God and the principles I had been taught. This became particularly apparent while I was attending school in Vancouver. Some fellow students organized gatherings supposedly to socialize or discuss school, but I knew that drinking alcohol and getting drunk would be a major part of these activities. While attending might have helped me develop friendships and academic connections, I chose not to participate because I did not feel comfortable placing myself in that environment. This decision sometimes left me feeling isolated, especially when I struggled to connect with other students. However, I believed that remaining faithful to my religious convictions was more important than gaining social acceptance. These experiences taught me that morality sometimes requires personal sacrifice, and that following my conscience and my faith may not always be the easiest or most socially rewarding choice.

As traditional Christian values become less widely accepted in some parts of society, I believe the best way to remain faithful to my beliefs is to defend freedom of conscience, equal justice under the law, and the rights of others to live according to their convictions, whether they believe in God or not. I do not believe it is possible or even desirable for governments to eliminate every immoral behavior through legislation. Instead, I believe a moral society must protect individual liberty while holding people accountable when their actions violate the rights and safety of others. Having grown up in Canada, I have come to admire the United States for its ongoing, though imperfect, efforts to live up to the principles expressed in its founding documents. Leaders such as Martin Luther King Jr. demonstrated how religious faith and moral conviction could inspire Americans to demand that their nation fulfill its promises of liberty and equality. Although the United States has often fallen short of these ideals, its history also demonstrates the progress that can occur when people work together to correct injustice. I believe that protecting freedom of conscience is essential to continuing that progress. Rather than responding to those who disagree with my beliefs through hostility or attempting to force my values upon them, I hope to live by example, defend their freedoms alongside my own, and encourage the honesty, compassion, accountability, and mutual respect that allow a free society to thrive.
